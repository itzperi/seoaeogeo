## 2. Review requests and replies

### The rules we follow

- **Ask for an honest review.** Never ask for a "5-star" review or tell people what to write.
- **No incentives.** No discounts, gifts, fee waivers or favours in return for a review.
- **No scripted wording.** Do not give clients sample text to paste.
- **Never review-gate.** Ask every client, not only the ones you think are happy. Do not first ask "were you satisfied?" and then send the link only to people who say yes. Google's policy forbids this.
- **Staff and family do not review the firm.**
- **Do not write reviews on a client's behalf** or post them from office devices.
- **It is fine to suggest they mention the service they used** (for example "company registration" or "GST registration"), because that helps other people understand what the review is about. Leave the rest to them.

```
Is a Private Limited Company right for your business?

A Private Limited Company is a separate legal entity. Its owners (shareholders) have limited liability, and it is often the preferred structure for businesses that plan to bring in investors or grow a team.

What you need to start:
- At least 2 directors and 2 shareholders (the same people can be both)
- At least one director who meets the Indian residency requirement
- Identity and address proof for each director
- Proof of the registered office address
- Digital Signature Certificates

Things to plan before you file:
- The proposed name and a backup option
- The main business activity
- How the shares will be split between founders

Running a Private Limited Company also brings yearly duties: board meetings, statutory audit, and annual filings with the Registrar of Companies.
```

```
Thinking about a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)?

An LLP combines the flexibility of a partnership with limited liability for its partners. Many professional firms, consultancies and family businesses choose it.

Key points:
- At least 2 designated partners, with at least one meeting the Indian residency requirement
- No minimum capital requirement; partners decide their contribution
- An LLP Agreement that sets out each partner's rights, duties and profit share. It must be filed with the MCA after incorporation within the prescribed time.
- Yearly filings: Statement of Account & Solvency and the Annual Return, plus income tax returns

LLP or Private Limited? If you plan to raise equity funding, a Private Limited Company is usually easier for investors. If you want a simpler structure for partners sharing profits, an LLP may suit you better.
```

**Button:** Learn more → `https://www.csrushil.com/opc-registration-chennai`

```
Running your business alone but want limited liability? Look at the One Person Company (OPC).

An OPC lets a single founder own a company with limited liability. Key points:
- One member and at least one director (the member can be the director)
- A nominee must be named, who takes over if the member is unable to continue
- The member and nominee must be natural persons who are Indian citizens, and meet the residency condition
- Fewer formalities than a Private Limited Company, but annual filings and audit still apply

An OPC can later be converted into a Private Limited Company when you bring in partners or investors.

Is an OPC right for you? It depends on your plans for growth, funding and tax. Visit our page to see the requirements and talk to our team.
```

```
Do you need GST registration? Here is how to tell.
```

```
What are ROC compliances, and why do they matter?

Every company and LLP registered with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs must file certain forms with the Registrar of Companies (ROC). Common ones include:
```

```
Need a Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)?
```

```
Deducting TDS? Filing the return on time matters as much as paying it.

If your business deducts tax at source on salaries, rent, professional fees, contract payments or other specified payments, you must:

2. File quarterly TDS returns (for example Form 24Q for salaries and Form 26Q for other payments)

Common mistakes we see:
- Wrong PAN of the deductee
- Mismatch between the challan and the return
```

## 7. ICAI check-list

This section is a list of **questions to confirm**, not legal conclusions. The ICAI Code of Ethics, the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 (including Clauses (6) and (7) of Part I of the First Schedule, which deal with solicitation and advertisement) and ICAI's guidelines on advertisement, websites and publicity change from time to time.

**Where to check:** ICAI website `https://www.icai.org` (look for the Code of Ethics and the Ethical Standards Board announcements). If in doubt, write to ICAI's Ethical Standards Board or seek a professional opinion before acting. Keep the written replies on file.

### Reviews and testimonials
1. May a CA firm ask clients to leave a review on Google? Does the answer change if the request is sent by WhatsApp, email or a desk QR code?
2. May the firm reply publicly to reviews, and what must replies avoid (for example any detail that could identify a client)?
3. May the website or social media display Google reviews or ratings?

### Google Business Profile and directories
4. Are free directory listings (Justdial, Sulekha, IndiaMART, Bing, Apple, LinkedIn) permitted, and what information may they contain?
5. Are paid or "premium" directory listings, sponsored placement or "verified/top" badges permitted?
6. Are lead-generation plans (paying a platform per enquiry) permitted?
7. Are there limits on the categories, keywords or service names used in listings?

### Paid advertising
8. Are paid search ads (Google Ads), paid map listings or boosted social media posts permitted for a CA firm?
9. If anything is permitted, what restrictions apply to the wording, for example on fees, comparisons or claims?

### Social media and content
10. Are educational posts, videos and YouTube Shorts by a member or firm permitted? Any rules on how the firm's name, logo or services are shown?
11. May the firm publish its services list and contact details on social media, as it does on its website?
12. Are there rules on mentioning qualifications (for example FAFD, Start-up Mentor) or team size?

### Talks, events and media
13. May a member offer free clinics, talks or workshops to associations, incubators and coworking spaces? Does offering it by email count as solicitation?
14. Are there conditions on being quoted in the media, such as how the member's name and firm may be described?

### Separate company structure
15. If a separate company or LLP were formed to offer non-attest services (for example business support or compliance), what does ICAI permit about its name, ownership, management, and its relationship with the CA firm?
16. Would that separate entity be subject to the same advertising and solicitation rules, and could it run ads or paid listings that the CA firm cannot?
17. Are there any registration or approval requirements with ICAI before forming such an entity?

### Fees
18. Are there rules on publishing fees, "starting from" prices or package prices online or in listings? (This kit deliberately avoids fees until this is confirmed.)
