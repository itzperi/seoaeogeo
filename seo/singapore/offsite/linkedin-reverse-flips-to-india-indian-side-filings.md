# Reverse flips to India: what the Indian-side filings involve

A reverse flip moves the holding company of a business from outside India, here Singapore, back to India. The scheme that does this is a legal exercise needing lawyers in both countries. This article is about something narrower: the Indian-side filings that sit around such a move, so that finance teams and founders know which work is tax, FEMA, accounting and ROC.

Public reports have covered moves of this kind by Flipkart (Reuters, 9 March 2026), Pine Labs (Inc42, 10 April 2025), Zepto (PTI, 28 January 2025) and Udaan (Entrackr, 18 March 2026). I make no comment on any of them.

C S Rushil & Co. does Indian tax, FEMA reporting, accounting and ROC work. We do not draft or run the scheme. Singapore tax, IRAS, ACRA and Singapore company law stay with the client's Singapore adviser. This is general information on Indian rules, and every structure has to be checked on its facts.

## What are the Indian-side pieces?

**Answer first:** four areas of Indian work surround a flip: FEMA reporting of the share movements, Indian tax on any gain and on payments, transfer pricing and annual reporting, and company law filings.

### 1. FEMA reporting of the share movements

A change in who owns the Indian company is reported to the Reserve Bank. Moving an existing Indian company under a new holding company involves share transfers, and each has its own FEMA pricing and reporting. A transfer of shares between a resident and a person resident outside India is reported on FC-TRS within 60 days of the transfer or of receipt or remittance of funds, whichever is earlier. A fresh allotment to a non-resident is reported on FC-GPR within 30 days of the allotment, and the allotment itself must be made within 60 days of receiving the money.

The reverse position also matters. If the Indian company has made overseas investment, or held foreign investment that exits during the flip, the annual FLA return can still fall due. A company whose foreign investor exited during the year, with nothing outstanding now, still files if foreign investment was outstanding a year earlier. The return is due on 15 July.

### 2. Resident shareholders and employee options

**Answer first:** Indian residents who hold shares or options in the Singapore company have their own FEMA position, and it needs checking before the scheme is filed.

Under the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022, where a resident's foreign securities are transferred because of a merger or the liquidation of the foreign entity, rule 17(3) requires approval of the competent authority under Indian or host-country law. Schedule III also lets a resident employee of an Indian company acquire shares of the overseas entity under an employee stock option plan offered globally on a uniform basis, with reporting by the Indian employer entity. What happens to those options when the Singapore company merges is a plan-terms and tax question, so the plan documents should be read early.

### 3. Indian tax on gains, and on payments

The tax result of a flip depends on the structure, which is one reason the scheme needs lawyers. In a merger of the Singapore company into an Indian company, a transfer in the scheme of amalgamation is not treated as a transfer where the amalgamated company is an Indian company (section 70(1)(e) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which corresponds to section 47(vi) of the 1961 Act), and shareholders who receive shares of that company are covered by section 70(1)(f). The conditions matter, so tax advice comes before the filing. Where a Singapore company holds shares of an Indian company, three treaty points come up.

- **Acquisition date.** Under Article 13 of the India-Singapore treaty, gains on Indian shares acquired before 1 April 2017 are taxable only in Singapore, subject to the limitation of benefits clause in Article 24A. Gains on shares acquired on or after 1 April 2017 may be taxed in India.
- **Limitation of benefits.** Article 24A can deny the benefit to a shell or conduit. It deems a company a shell if its annual expenditure on operations in Singapore is below S$200,000, tested for each 12-month period in the 24 months before the gain arises, with safe harbours for a company listed on a recognised Singapore exchange or one that meets that figure.
- **Residence certificates are not conclusive.** In January 2026 the Supreme Court held in Tiger Global (Civil Appeal No. 262 of 2026) that a tax residency certificate is an eligibility condition, not sufficient evidence of residence, and the Delhi bench of the Tribunal applied Article 24A in Hareon Solar Singapore the same month. The general anti-avoidance rule and the multilateral convention's principal purpose test also apply.

Payments between the Indian company and the Singapore company during the transition need their own check. Tax is deducted under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 where the payment is taxable. The remittance is reported in Form 145, with Form 146 where a Chartered Accountant's certificate is required, and treaty relief needs the Singapore company's certificate of residence from IRAS and Form 41 (Form 10F for payments in financial year 2025-26 and earlier).

### 4. Transfer pricing, accounts and company law

Until the flip is complete, the Indian company keeps its group transactions. Any international transaction with an associated enterprise needs the accountant's report: Form 3CEB for financial year 2025-26, by 31 October 2026, and Form 48 under section 172 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from tax year 2026-27, first due by 31 October 2027. Accounts continue on India's April to March year, and the Registrar of Companies filings follow the changes. Further filings with the Registrar follow the merger order, and we list their forms and dates from the order itself.

## What stays with whom?

- **Lawyers in both countries:** the scheme itself, and any court or tribunal process.
- **The Singapore adviser:** Singapore tax, IRAS, ACRA and Singapore company law.
- **An Indian Chartered Accountant:** Indian tax, FEMA reporting, accounting, the transfer pricing report and ROC filings.

A useful first step for any team is a single dated list of every Indian filing triggered by each step of the scheme, agreed with both sets of advisers before the first share moves.
