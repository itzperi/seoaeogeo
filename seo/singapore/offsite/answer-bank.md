## S1. How do I set up a subsidiary in India from Singapore?

I am a CA at an Indian firm. A Singapore company can usually own all of an Indian private limited company if the sector is under the automatic route and within any sectoral limit. The company needs at least two shareholders, so the parent normally holds all but one share and a nominee holds that one. At least one director must have stayed in India for 182 days in the financial year, though the Singapore directors can join board meetings remotely.

The first dates come quickly. The first auditor is appointed within 30 days of incorporation. The parent remits the subscription money, shares are allotted within 60 days of receipt, and Form FC-GPR is filed with the RBI within 30 days of the allotment. Form INC-20A is due within 180 days of incorporation. Singapore and India are both parties to the Apostille Convention, so documents signed in Singapore are usually notarised and apostilled.

The parent's own ACRA and IRAS position, and Singapore company law, are for your Singapore adviser.

## S2. What does a Singapore-owned Indian subsidiary have to file each year?

I am a CA at an Indian firm. The subsidiary follows India's April to March year, whatever the parent's year-end. Monthly, TDS is deposited by the 7th of the next month, and GST returns follow the company's scheme. Advance tax is paid in four instalments, by 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March. The FLA return goes to the RBI by 15 July.

Company law dates run from the AGM, held by 30 September. Form AOC-4 follows within 30 days and the full Form MGT-7 within 60 days. A subsidiary cannot use the shorter MGT-7A. Where the company has international transactions with its parent, the transfer pricing report is due by 31 October and the income tax return by 30 November. GSTR-9 is due by 31 December where required. The board must meet at least four times a year, with no more than 120 days between meetings.

Event-driven filings, such as FC-GPR after an allotment, are additional. Singapore filings stay with your Singapore adviser.

## S3. Form 3CEB due date for an Indian subsidiary of a Singapore company

I am a CA at an Indian firm. For financial year 2025-26 the report was due on 31 October 2026, with the income tax return due on 30 November 2026 for companies that must file it. The September 2026 extension covered tax audit reports and returns without transfer pricing, but not Form 3CEB. If it was missed, file it now. For FY 2025-26 and earlier, a penalty of Rs 1,00,000 can apply unless there was reasonable cause.

Any international transaction with an associated enterprise needs the report, whatever its value. A parent's cost recharge or a software fee is enough. The date follows the Indian company's April to March year, not the parent's year-end.

From tax year 2026-27, Form 3CEB is replaced by Form 48 under section 172 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. It is first due by 31 October 2027. A Chartered Accountant in practice certifies the report.

Your Singapore adviser handles the parent's own Singapore tax return.

## S4. India-Singapore DTAA withholding tax on royalties and fees for technical services

I am a CA at an Indian firm. Under the India-Singapore treaty, Indian tax on royalties and fees for technical services is capped at 10% where the Singapore company is the beneficial owner. A lower rate under India's own law still applies if it is lower, so the treaty rate is a ceiling.

Two cautions. First, fees for technical services has a narrow treaty definition. A management or consultancy fee counts only if, for example, it makes technical knowledge or skill available to the payer. Other fees are generally business profits, taxable in India only through a permanent establishment. Second, a permanent establishment can arise if the Singapore company's staff furnish services in India for more than 90 days in a fiscal year, or 30 days where the services are for a related enterprise.

To claim the rate, the Indian payer normally needs the IRAS Certificate of Residence, Form 41 (Form 10F for payments in FY 2025-26 and earlier), and beneficial ownership and no-PE declarations. The Singapore company applies to IRAS for the certificate, with its Singapore adviser.

## S5. Reverse flip from Singapore to India: steps and tax

I am a CA at an Indian firm. I cannot give a step list without knowing the structure, because a reverse flip is a legal scheme that needs lawyers in both countries. What I can describe is the Indian-side work around it.

Share movements are reported to the RBI. A transfer between a resident and a non-resident is reported on FC-TRS within 60 days, and a fresh allotment to a non-resident on FC-GPR within 30 days of the allotment. The annual FLA return can still fall due while foreign investment is outstanding.

On tax, a Singapore company's gains on Indian shares depend on when the shares were acquired. Under Article 13 of the treaty, shares acquired before 1 April 2017 are taxable only in Singapore, subject to the limitation of benefits clause in Article 24A. Shares acquired on or after that date may be taxed in India. A residence certificate alone is not conclusive.

Singapore tax, IRAS and ACRA stay with your Singapore adviser. Please take a written structure to both sets of advisers before any share moves.

## S6. I live in Singapore and want to sell my flat in Chennai: how much TDS?

I am a CA at an Indian firm. If you are a non-resident, the buyer must deduct TDS on the whole sale price, not on your profit. For property held over 24 months, the rate is 12.5% plus surcharge and cess. On a sale of Rs 1.2 crore, that is Rs 15 lakh before surcharge and cess.

You can reduce this by applying on TRACES for a lower or nil deduction certificate once the sale agreement is signed. The certificate names the buyer and sets the rate. A flat bought for Rs 70 lakh and sold for Rs 1.2 crore gives a gain of Rs 50 lakh, and the tax on that gain at 12.5% is Rs 6.25 lakh, before surcharge and cess.

The buyer needs a TAN, because the seller is a non-resident. The proceeds go to your NRO account. To send them abroad you need Form 15CA and, where required, Form 15CB (Forms 145 and 146 from 1 April 2026). The limit is USD 1 million per financial year.

Singapore's treatment is for your Singapore adviser.

## S7. Do NRIs in Singapore need to file an income tax return in India?

I am a CA at an Indian firm. India taxes a non-resident only on income earned or received in India, such as rent from a flat, NRO interest, capital gains on Indian shares or property, and Indian business income. Your Singapore salary is not taxed in India while you are a non-resident and receive it outside India. If it is credited into an Indian account, it can be treated as received in India.

Whether a return is compulsory depends on your Indian income and facts I cannot judge from a post. In practice, a return is how you set the TDS deducted from your rent or interest against the tax actually due, and claim a refund of any excess.

Your status depends on days in India, not on your passport or OCI card. Broadly, 182 days in a tax year makes you resident, and a second test counts days across the year and the four years before it. Keep your travel dates from your passport.

Singapore tax is for your Singapore adviser.

## S8. Moving back to India from Singapore: what happens to my CPF and RSUs for Indian tax?

I am a CA at an Indian firm. Singapore side first, as reported by IRAS and the CPF Board. CPF can be closed only when you are no longer a citizen or permanent resident. SRS withdrawals by foreigners carry 24% withholding. Your employer will file Form IR21 and may hold your final pay until IRAS clears it. Your Singapore adviser should decide the timing.

For India, status is fixed for each tax year. A returning NRI is often RNOR, which generally keeps foreign income outside Indian tax, unless it comes from a business controlled in India. Whether a CPF or SRS payout is income, and how, depends on what the payment represents, so read the statement first.

RSUs are taxed as salary when they vest. The part earned from work done in India is Indian income whatever your status. The part earned in Singapore is generally taxed in India only when you are resident and ordinarily resident, or if received in India. Keep a record of your working days in each country.

Redesignate NRE accounts when you return.

## S9. A chartered accountant in India for a Singapore company's Indian subsidiary

I am a CA at an Indian firm. Rather than a name, I would suggest asking for a written scope. An Indian subsidiary usually needs ROC filings, RBI reporting (FC-GPR and the FLA return), income tax and TDS, GST, a statutory audit, and, if it deals with its parent, a transfer pricing report certified by a Chartered Accountant in practice. One firm may do all of it or several may share it, so ask who prepares each filing and who signs.

Useful questions: is there a dated calendar that names the owner of each date? Who coordinates with your Singapore adviser on figures and timing? Which filings need your resident director's signature? Can the firm explain, in plain terms, what happens when a date is missed?

You can also check any Indian CA's membership with ICAI before you engage them.

Remember the split. An Indian adviser handles Indian law. Your Singapore adviser keeps the parent's ACRA, IRAS and Singapore company law work, and the two should share dates early.

## S10. Our India subsidiary's compliance keeps getting flagged by auditors: who handles statutory filings?

I am a CA at an Indian firm. The auditor reviews the accounts but does not prepare your filings. Statutory filings are prepared by the company's accounts team, Chartered Accountant or company secretary, and signed by the directors, usually including the resident director.

The filings that tend to be flagged are the ones no monthly routine prompts: FC-GPR after a further allotment, the FLA return due on 15 July, a transfer pricing report where the only dealing with the parent is a cost recharge, DIR-3 KYC for directors abroad, TDS on payments to the parent, and INC-20A in a new company.

Several can be regularised late. FC-GPR and FLA have a late submission fee route for a limited period, and a late DIR-3 KYC costs Rs 5,000. Interest runs on late TDS.

A practical fix is one dated calendar with a named owner for each filing, plus a list of past gaps. Your Singapore adviser keeps the parent's own filings, and the two advisers should agree the dates.
