# Indian tax for NRIs in the Gulf: six questions to settle before you file

Every year the same handful of questions decides whether an Indian return filed from Riyadh, Doha, Muscat, Kuwait City, Manama or Dubai is right. None of them is difficult on its own. The trouble is that they interact: your day count decides your status, your status decides what India taxes, and your country of residence decides whether a treaty can help at all.

This is general information on Indian law. Gulf rules on residence permits, end-of-service benefits and local taxes stay with your employer or local adviser.

## 1. Is my Gulf salary taxed in India?

**Short answer:** not while you are a non-resident and the salary is for work done in the Gulf and received outside India.

Two things can change that. The first is where the money lands. Salary credited straight into an Indian account can be treated as received in India, so route it to your Gulf account first. The second is your residential status, which is tested afresh every financial year (question 2).

## 2. Am I still a non-resident this year?

**Short answer:** count your days in India; do not estimate them.

You are resident for a year if you spend 182 days or more in India, or 60 days or more in the year together with 365 days or more over the four years before it. For an Indian citizen or a person of Indian origin who is visiting, the 60-day figure becomes 120 days where Indian income is above Rs 15 lakh.

There is a second test that matters more in the Gulf than almost anywhere else. An Indian citizen whose Indian income is above Rs 15 lakh, and who is not liable to tax in any other country by reason of residence or domicile, is treated as resident, though only as "resident but not ordinarily resident" (RNOR). Most Gulf states do not tax personal salary, so this deemed-residency rule needs a fresh look each year.

Keep a simple travel log from your passport stamps or e-gate record, your residence permit (iqama, Qatar ID, resident card, civil ID, CPR card or Emirates ID), your employment contract and payslips. They are the evidence if the department ever asks.

## 3. Which of my Indian income is taxed, and why file if tax was already deducted?

**Short answer:** India taxes income that arises in India, and filing is how you get back excess tax deducted at source.

Rent from a flat in India, interest on NRO deposits, gains on Indian shares, mutual funds and property, and Indian business income are all taxable. Interest on NRE and FCNR deposits is exempt while you are a non-resident.

Banks deduct tax on NRO interest at the flat rate for non-residents, whatever your slab. Where your total Indian income is modest, that deduction often exceeds the tax actually due, and the only way to recover the difference is to file a return. Before filing, match every credit against Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement, and check that rent collected by a relative on your behalf is reported in your name.

## 4. Can I use a tax treaty, and do I need Form 10F?

**Short answer:** only if India has a treaty with your country and you can prove you are its tax resident.

India has double taxation avoidance agreements with the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman and Kuwait. **It has no such agreement with Bahrain.** The only India-Bahrain agreement listed by the Income Tax Department is a Tax Information Exchange Agreement (Notification No. 44/2013, in force from 11 April 2013), which lets the two tax authorities share information but does not decide which country taxes what. A resident of Bahrain is therefore taxed in India under Indian law alone, with no treaty rate to claim.

Where a treaty does exist, the claim needs a tax residency certificate from the other country's tax authority, plus the prescribed information filed on the Indian e-filing portal. For 2025-26 and earlier years that is Form 10F. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which applies from tax year 2026-27, the same information is given in Form 41, under section 159(8).

The practical catch in the Gulf is the certificate itself. Some authorities issue one to a salaried individual and some may not, and the wording matters. Ask early. If no certificate can be obtained, plan the return without the treaty rather than assume relief.

## 5. I am selling a flat in India. How much tax will the buyer deduct?

**Short answer:** usually far more than your real tax, unless you get a lower-deduction certificate first.

When the seller is a non-resident, the buyer must obtain a TAN, deduct tax and deposit it. The deduction is worked out on the sale price, not on your gain. For property held for more than 24 months, the long-term rate is 12.5% plus surcharge and cess, applied to the whole consideration.

The remedy is an application to the income tax department for a lower or nil deduction certificate before the sale is registered. It is supported by the sale agreement, purchase and improvement costs, a capital gains computation and your return history. The certificate names the buyer and the rate. Build the time it takes into your registration plans.

To send the proceeds abroad, the bank will ask for the remittance forms: Form 15CA and the accountant's certificate in Form 15CB, which become Forms 145 and 146 under the 2025 Act. Remittances from NRO balances and sale proceeds are limited to USD 1 million per financial year.

## 6. I am moving back. What should I plan before I resign?

**Short answer:** the month you arrive can decide how long your foreign income stays outside Indian tax.

A returning NRI is often "resident but not ordinarily resident" for a period: broadly, if you were a non-resident in nine of the ten preceding years, or spent 729 days or fewer in India in the seven preceding years. While RNOR, foreign income generally stays outside Indian tax, unless it comes from a business controlled in India or a profession set up in India.

End-of-service benefits are paid after you leave, and their Indian treatment depends on your status in the year you receive them and on what the payment is for. Work this out before you hand in your notice. Redesignate NRE accounts when you return, and expect foreign assets to need disclosure once you become ordinarily resident.

## A short checklist

- Count your India days for the year from your passport.
- Keep salary out of Indian accounts.
- Test deemed residency if your Indian income is above Rs 15 lakh.
- Reconcile NRO interest TDS with Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement.
- Claim a treaty only with a residence certificate, and never for Bahrain.
- Apply for a lower-deduction certificate before a property sale is registered.

Due dates and forms change, so check the current notifications on the Income Tax Department's portal before you file.
