## 0. Method and limits

- WebSearch is a US-based tool, so the orders below are not a local SERP (not Google.sg, .ae, .de or .nl). Order is as returned.
- WebFetch was blocked for almost every domain ("unable to verify domain", one DNS failure). I fell back to plain HTTP fetches. Blocked (403) and not read: Toptal, Clutch, Kees de Boekhouder, Accountor, Sifted. Unreachable: expatax.nl. Sleek's CFO page returned 404 on direct fetch, so Sleek facts are from search snippets only.
- Word counts are approximate (scripts, nav and footer stripped). JSON-LD types are from page source.
- Not observable: which sources AI answers cite. No AI query tool was available, so the third-party list below is inferred from SERPs.
- Canada and Australia virtual CFO queries were not in the brief's query list and were not run. US parents were covered only through the India queries; no US-local firm surfaced.

## 2. Germany and Netherlands: rules a bookkeeper must design around

## 3. Gap analysis against our site

Existing Singapore and UAE entries (src/lib/bookkeeping-markets.ts) are ahead of most pages read: each has a hand-off table, hours table, 3 to 5 sourced rules and a data-transfer note. UAE covers e-invoicing and audited accounts, which BCL covers in prose only.

Gaps:
- UAE: no WPS or free-zone licence-type content, which Meydan (free zone) and RSM address. Meydan ends with a References section; our per-row citations are equal or better.
- Singapore: no page-level answer to "reporting to the overseas parent", the one H2 Dezan Shira uses. SFRS appears only in a hand-off row.
- Neither entry says whether local law restricts where books may be kept. Germany does (above). Check Singapore and UAE before the next edit; unchecked here.
- Germany and the Netherlands have no entries, which is correct until the rules above are closed.

Virtual CFO page (https://www.csrushil.com/virtual-cfo-services): 5,195 words, 2 tables, FAQPage, HowTo and Service JSON-LD, most H2 are questions. Longer and better structured than every competitor read. Weakness for these markets: Singapore appears once; Germany, treaty and Form 15CA not at all, so a Singapore or German parent cannot see its own case. Its foreign-parent section covers board packs, group mapping, FEMA (FC-GPR, FLA) and Form 3CEB.

India subsidiary page (https://www.csrushil.com/india-subsidiary-compliance-cfo): 3,703 words, FEMA 9 mentions, transfer pricing 22, treaty 0. Has a 12-month calendar and monthly parent pack; never mentions treaty paperwork or remittance certificates. Check wording: the virtual CFO page says Form 3CEB, while locked rule 9 says Form 3CEB became Form 48 (section 172) under the 2025 Act from 1 April 2026. For 2025-26 year-end reports the old form may still be right, so state both clearly.

## 4. Outline: one cross-border guide

Working title: "Finance function for a foreign-owned startup in India: virtual CFO, MIS and investor reporting". One URL. Needs owner approval first (locked rule 10: new service or page).

1. What an Indian subsidiary's finance function produces each month: close, MIS, parent pack, investor pack. Anchor to the existing monthly pack and add only what is new.
2. What stays with whom: our side (Indian books, MIS, FEMA filing data, Indian statutory calendar); the parent's home adviser (home tax, company law, filings). Reuse the existing "not included" logic.
3. One table of what differs by parent country (facts below). Not eight sections.
4. Remittances and treaty paperwork on the Indian side: paying the parent for services, royalties, dividends and interest, and what the bank asks for.
5. Reporting calendar: India year end of 31 March against the parent's close, with a sample bridge.
6. Investor reporting for a VC-backed startup: board pack, cap table tie-out, FC-GPR timing.
7. FAQ of 6 to 8 questions, written only from real client questions.

Indian-side facts that differ by parent country, and how to verify each:
- Treaty (DTAA) rates and conditions on dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services differ per treaty. Verify on incometaxindia.gov.in (treaty list) and the treaty text. NOT VERIFIED in this session.
- Tax residency certificate, Form 10F, and Forms 15CA and 15CB: the same Indian forms for every country, but the rate certified differs. Verify on incometax.gov.in; check section numbers against the Act 2025 mapping post before writing any.
- Transfer pricing: Indian rules are the same for every parent. The parent's own documentation (US, UK, Germany, Netherlands each have one) belongs to the home adviser and is only named.
- Parent accounting framework and close dates differ (US GAAP or IFRS, FRS 102, HGB or IFRS, Dutch GAAP, AASB, ASPE). Confirm with the parent's group finance, not a website.
- FEMA: FC-GPR and FLA are the same for every parent; sector and route rules apply by sector, not by country. Verify on rbi.org.in. NOT VERIFIED in this session.

Doorway test: one URL, no per-country sections, no "CA in X" text. The country table carries only facts that really differ; everything else is shared and written once.

## 6. Adopt, Adapt, Avoid (locked rules)

Adopt
- Primary-source citations in the page body (Meydan's References, firma.de's statute labels). Ours already cite per row.
- One "obligations at a glance" table with a deadline column (Intercompany Solutions).
- A reporting-to-parent heading (Dezan Shira), written from our own monthly pack.

Adapt
- Marketplace presence (Clutch, Toptal): do not chase ratings or sponsorship; publish the verifiable "who files what" tables the marketplaces lack.
- firma.de's partner-firm model: state that the client's local adviser files; no price.
- FD Capital and Standard Ledger stage structure: use stages without package names or prices.
- FAQPage with 6 to 9 genuine questions (Standard Ledger; our India subsidiary page already does this).

Avoid
- Prices, price bands, "from" amounts (Sleek, firma.de, BCL, LedgersCFO).
- Superlatives and ranking claims ("Best Accounting Firm", "Trusted by over 2,000", "80+ startups").
- Client counts, logos, ratings, AggregateRating.
- Per-country near-duplicate pages (Toptal-style city variants).
- Copying text, headings or tables from any page read.
- Implying Indian-hosted books are acceptable for a German entity without § 146(2b) approval.
