> Moved back to Chennai after years abroad? Your foreign income may not be taxable in India straight away.
>
> Many returning NRIs qualify as "resident but not ordinarily resident" (RNOR) for a transition period. During that time, foreign income earned outside India and not linked to India generally stays outside Indian tax, and Schedule FA reporting isn't required. Status is worked out year by year from your past years of non-residence and the days you spent in India, so the travel record matters.
>
> Once you become ordinarily resident, worldwide income and foreign assets (bank accounts, RSUs, pensions, property) are taxable and must be reported every year.
>
> Families in Besant Nagar often bring returns that mix foreign pensions, RSUs, rental income and property or share sales. Our page for Besant Nagar residents explains how residency, foreign income and capital gains fit together. Meetings are at our Anna Nagar office or online.

> GST appeal against a Coimbatore order? The GST Appellate Tribunal now has a Tamil Nadu State Bench sitting in Coimbatore.
>
> A GST dispute moves up a fixed ladder:
> 1. Reply to the show-cause notice, usually within 30 days.
> 2. First appeal in Form APL-01 within three months of the order, with a pre-deposit of 10% of the disputed tax.
> 3. Second appeal to the GSTAT, with a further pre-deposit of 10%.
>
> Tamil Nadu's GSTAT benches are at Chennai, Coimbatore and Madurai. The GSTAT e-filing portal assigns the bench when you file, so check it there rather than assuming the nearest city.
>
> A practising Chartered Accountant is an authorised representative before GST officers, the Appellate Authority and the GSTAT under Section 116 of the CGST Act.
>

### Post 3 · week of 27 Oct · Private limited company (Coimbatore)

## 8. Reviews: request template and reply guidance

### WhatsApp request (send from the firm's number, +91 72001 49711)

```
Hello [first name], thank you for working with C S Rushil & Co. If you are comfortable doing so, we would value your feedback on Google. It is entirely optional. Here is the link:

```

It offers no incentive and suggests no wording, keywords or star rating. Don't edit it to add any of these.

**Asking at the right moment**
- Ask only **existing clients**, and only **after a piece of work is complete**: a return filed and acknowledged, a notice reply submitted, an appeal heard, a company incorporated (certificate issued), or a refund received. Never ask before the outcome, during a dispute, or while fees are outstanding.
- Send one request, with no reminders beyond a single gentle follow-up after 7 days, if any.
- Spread requests out. The playbook's target of 2–3 a week, every week, is right. Don't send a batch to a list, which is what created the earlier burst pattern.
- Don't give clients any draft text, even when they ask "what should I write?". Reply "whatever reflects your experience". The old template-filled review ("[Income tax filing/GST compliance]") shows the risk.
- The playbook suggests naming the work in your own words when you ask (for example, "now that the GST appeal is filed…"). That's fine as context, but don't suggest what the client should say.
- Never review the firm from staff, family or friends' accounts, and never pay for or exchange reviews. Don't ask clients to remove negative reviews.
- ICAI note: the ICAI Code of Ethics restricts solicitation by members. Keep requests limited to existing clients, after the work is done, with no incentive. Don't post or forward reviews in advertising. If the firm wants certainty, it should check current ICAI guidance on online reviews.

**Replying to reviews (within 48 hours)**
- Thank the reviewer by first name (or the name shown). Keep it to 1–3 sentences in a professional tone.
- **Never confirm that the person is a client, and never mention their matter, amounts, notices, refunds, company names or dates.** Professional confidentiality applies even when the reviewer has disclosed details themselves.
- You may refer to the *type* of work in general terms only if the reviewer named it first, for example: "Thank you, Priya. We're glad the process was clear."
- Don't use promotional lines ("we are the best…", "call us for…"), prices or offers.
- **Negative reviews:** stay calm, factual and brief. Don't discuss the matter publicly. For example: "Thank you for the feedback. We take every concern seriously. Please contact us on +91 72001 49711 or admin@csrushil.com so we can look into it." If a review is fake or breaks Google's policies, report it through the profile, without arguing in the reply.
- Rotate replies so they aren't identical (the earlier replies were nearly all "Thank you for the wonderful feedback").
